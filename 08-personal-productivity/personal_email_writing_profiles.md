Analysis Method
Analyze email and work chat separately.
Do not merge the channels into one general profile.
For each channel, evaluate the following dimensions.
1. Tone
Identify recurring tone characteristics, such as:
• formal or informal;
• direct or indirect;
• warm or neutral;
• concise or explanatory;
• assertive or cautious;
• collaborative or instructional;
• polite, apologetic, urgent, analytical, or conversational.
Explain how the tone changes depending on:

• seniority;
• internal versus external communication;
• urgency;
• request type;
• disagreement;
• follow-up;
• status reporting;
• technical discussion.
2. Sentence Structure
Analyze:
• typical sentence length;
• simple versus complex sentences;
• use of fragments;
• paragraph length;
• frequency of questions;
• use of commands or requests;
• transitions between ideas;
• opening and closing structures;
• tendency to combine multiple points in one sentence;

3. Vocabulary

Evidence and Confidence
For every major conclusion:
• base it on repeated patterns rather than one isolated message;
• indicate whether confidence is high, medium, or low;
• distinguish stable habits from occasional behaviour;
• avoid overstating weak evidence.
Do not include exact message counts when doing so could reveal sensitive communication patterns, unless the counts are broad and anonymized.
